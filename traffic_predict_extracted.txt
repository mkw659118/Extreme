Abstract—Network traffic forecasting is essential for proactive
network operations such as capacity provisioning and traffic
engineering. In production networks, however, forecasting inputs
are often incomplete due to sampled flow records, sketch-
based counters, telemetry loss, throttling, and device or col-
lector failures. Meanwhile, low average prediction error does
not necessarily imply operationally useful forecasts, since SLA-
aware provisioning is sensitive to peak under-prediction and
traffic engineering depends on accurately capturing high-load OD
demands and traffic regimes. Existing methods usually assume
fully observed inputs, optimize pointwise errors, and apply a
globally shared predictor to heterogeneous traffic dynamics. This
paper proposes DATP-Net, an operation-aware traffic forecasting
framework for incomplete telemetry. DATP-Net jointly encodes
raw observations, temporal differences, and validity masks to
build a missing-aware trend representation. It estimates multi-
scale latent traffic-state distributions with Student-t mixture and
uses the fused state posterior to guide Top-K expert forecasting.
To compensate for missing temporal context, DATP-Net retrieves
similar historical patterns via mask-aware matching and fuses
them through a reliability-bounded gate. We further analyze how
forecasting errors affect SLA violation risk and maximum-link-
utilization degradation. Experiments on Abilene, G ´EANT, and
three hydrological datasets show that DATP-Net improves accu-
racy, trend preservation, robustness, and downstream operational
utility. It reduces short-horizon NMAE by up to 17.1%, improves
long-horizon COS by up to 81.6%, and remains robust under
structured time-block missingness, while achieving better SLA–
Cost trade-offs and MLU ratios closer to the ideal value.
Index Terms—Network traffic forecasting, distribution-aware
modeling, trend preservation, capacity provisioning, traffic engi-
neering.
I. INTRODUCTION
P
ROACTIVE network management increasingly relies on
accurate forecasts of future traffic. Traffic prediction sup-
ports traffic engineering [1], capacity planning [2], congestion
mitigation, load balancing, network slicing, and energy-aware
resource allocation [3], [4]. In these scenarios, a forecaster is
not an isolated time-series model. It serves as the front end
of a closed-loop control pipeline that converts raw measure-
ments into predictions and then into operational decisions. Its
practical value is therefore determined not only by statistical
accuracy, but also by the quality of the decisions enabled by its
forecasts. This raises a key question: what properties should a
traffic forecaster have to be operationally useful under realistic
network conditions?
We argue that answering this question requires addressing
three networking-specific challenges that are not jointly han-
dled by existing forecasting models.
First, network telemetry is intrinsically incomplete
and often structurally missing. Production measurement
systems continuously trade accuracy for overhead. Sampled
NetFlow/sFlow observes only a fraction of packets [5]–[9];
sketch-based counters compress per-flow states and may lose
information under memory pressure [10]; INT reports can be
dropped under congestion [11]; and device reboots, link flaps,
or collector failures often create contiguous missing blocks
rather than isolated random holes. A practical forecaster should
therefore work directly with incomplete telemetry and remain
robust to both random and structured missingness.
Second, low average prediction error does not necessar-
ily imply good network operation. In capacity provisioning,
under-prediction during traffic growth or peak periods is often
more harmful than small errors during low-load periods. An
over-smoothed forecast may achieve competitive MAE or
MSE, but still underestimate high-load traffic and cause SLA
violations unless excessive backup capacity is reserved [2].
Similarly, in traffic engineering, decision quality depends on
the joint demand matrix and on correctly identifying heavily
loaded OD pairs. A forecast that is accurate on average but
misestimates peak OD demands can still lead to a larger
maximum link utilization [12] (MLU). Thus, an operation-
aware forecaster should preserve traffic trends and explicitly
relate forecasting errors to downstream operational costs.
Third, network traffic is governed by multiple latent
regimes rather than a single stationary process. Traffic
dynamics are shaped by user behavior, application work-
loads, routing policies, diurnal periodicity, and sudden demand
shifts. Traffic windows may therefore alternate among low-
load states, periodic fluctuations, sustained growth or decline,

MAE, RMSE, or MAPE. This paradigm is insufficient for
operational network forecasting. Real telemetry can be in-

not translate into good SLA-aware provisioning or traffic

accuracy and operational usefulness.
To address these challenges, we propose DATP-Net, an
operation-aware, Distribution-Aware and Trend-Preserving
forecasting framework for incomplete network telemetry.
DATP-Net first estimates the latent traffic regime of each
input and then adapts its forecasting behavior accordingly. It
estimates multi-scale Student-t state posteriors and uses the
fused posterior to route inputs to specialized experts through a
Top-K mixture-of-experts mechanism. To preserve operation-
critical dynamics, DATP-Net encodes raw observations, tem-
poral differences, and validity masks, and constrains predicted
variations with a trend-consistency objective. To compensate
for missing context, it retrieves similar historical segments
via mask-aware matching and incorporates them through a
reliability-bounded fusion gate. We also provide an error-to-
cost analysis that relates forecasting errors to SLA violation
risk and MLU degradation, making operation-aware evaluation
explicit.

provisioning and traffic engineering, rather than treating fore-
casting as a standalone time-series task.
• Multi-scale distribution-aware expert routing. DATP-
Net estimates Student-t state posteriors at multiple temporal
scales and uses the fused posterior to guide a Top-K mix-
ture of forecasting experts, enabling adaptive prediction for
heterogeneous traffic regimes.

II. RELATED WORK
Network traffic forecasting has been widely studied with
deep sequence models, and many methods share foundations
with general time-series forecasting. Recurrent models are
among the earliest choices for modeling traffic evolution. For
example, NeuTM uses LSTM to predict future traffic matrices
from historical observations [15]. Recent recurrent variants
further enhance long-range modeling, such as xLSTM-based
forecasting models [16], [17] and P-sLSTM with patch-based
representations and structured recurrent memory [18]. These
methods capture temporal dependencies effectively, but their
prediction dynamics are usually shared across samples, making
it difficult to adapt explicitly to traffic windows governed by
different latent regimes.
Transformer-style models have become strong alternatives
for long-term forecasting. FEDformer combines decomposi-
tion and frequency-enhanced attention [19], PatchTST models
temporal patches in a channel-independent manner [20], and
iTransformer represents variables as tokens to capture multi-
variate dependencies [21]. More recent models, such as PMD-
former [22] and HMformer [23], further improve temporal
representation through patch-mean decoupling and hierarchical
multi-scale modeling. Although these models improve long-
range and multi-scale dependency modeling, their multi-scale
features are mainly used to enrich temporal representations
rather than to estimate latent traffic-state distributions and
guide adaptive prediction.
Convolutional, graph-based, and feature-mixing models pro-
vide complementary mechanisms for capturing local, spatial,
periodic, and time-frequency patterns. In network traffic fore-
casting, D-STN jointly models spatial and temporal depen-
dencies for mobile traffic prediction [24], while MVSTGN

---PAGE 3---
exploits multi-view graph structures to characterize heteroge-
neous spatial correlations [25]. For general time-series fore-
casting, TimesNet transforms one-dimensional sequences into
multi-period two-dimensional representations [26], WPMixer
combines wavelet decomposition with temporal patching and
feature mixing [27], and FeTS learns feature-aware temporal
representations from local segments [28]. These methods im-
prove representation learning from different perspectives, but
most still focus on minimizing pointwise errors and rarely
impose direct constraints on traffic evolution trends.
In summary, existing forecasting models have improved
temporal, spatial, and multi-scale representation learning, but
they commonly assume fully observed inputs, optimize av-
erage prediction errors, and apply a largely shared prediction
behavior to heterogeneous traffic dynamics. These assumptions
are limiting in practical networks, where telemetry is often
incomplete, traffic alternates among different regimes, and
downstream operations are sensitive to trend distortion and
high-load misprediction. DATP-Net addresses these limitations
through distribution-aware expert routing, missing-aware trend
preservation, and reliability-controlled historical enhancement,
all operating directly on incomplete telemetry.
III. OPERATION-AWARE PROBLEM FORMULATION
This section formulates traffic forecasting from incomplete
telemetry as an operation-aware problem. Unlike generic time-
series forecasting, the prediction is used by downstream net-
work operations, such as SLA-aware capacity provisioning and
traffic engineering. We therefore define the forecasting task,
the missingness model, and the operational costs induced by
prediction errors, and then derive theoretical observations that
motivate DATP-Net.

IV. METHODOLOGY
A. Overview
DATP-Net is designed for operation-aware traffic fore-
casting from incomplete telemetry. Instead of mapping all
traffic windows through one globally shared predictor, DATP-
Net first identifies the latent traffic regime of the current
input and then adapts the forecasting behavior accordingly.
Meanwhile, it explicitly encodes traffic evolution and uses
reliability-controlled historical evidence to compensate for

---PAGE 5---
the contextual information lost under missing observations.
This design addresses the four requirements in Section III:
missingness robustness, trend preservation, regime adaptivity,
and reliability-bounded context compensation.
As shown in Fig. 2, DATP-Net contains six components.
First, Missing-Aware Trend Encoding builds a unified rep-
resentation from raw traffic values, temporal differences,
and validity masks. Second, Multi-Scale Student’s t State
Estimation extracts scale-specific descriptors and estimates
their posterior probabilities over latent traffic states. Third,
Adaptive Multi-Scale State Fusion integrates state evidence
from different temporal scales into a unified routing vector.
Fourth, Distribution-Guided Top-K Expert Forecasting ac-
tivates only the most relevant forecasting experts according
to the fused state distribution. Fifth, Mask-Aware Historical
Pattern Enhancement retrieves similar historical windows
over jointly observed positions and produces a retrieval-based
prediction. Finally, Reliability-Bounded Prediction Fusion
combines the expert-based and historical predictions with a
bounded adaptive weight.
B. Missing-Aware Trend Encoding

tude but also temporal evolution. However, under incomplete
telemetry, directly computing temporal differences after zero
filling may introduce artificial jumps, which can be mistakenly
treated as bursts or turning points. DATP-Net therefore com-
putes trend features only when the required raw observations
are valid, and attaches a validity mask to each feature channel.

I. Training and Online Deployment
DATP-Net is trained in three stages, as summarized in
Algorithm 1. We first pretrain the missing-aware encoder and
Student’s t mixture to establish a latent traffic-state space. We
then jointly train the state estimator, router, and forecasting ex-
perts with the state-constrained forecasting objective. Finally,
after the backbone is fixed, we build the historical memory
from the training set and fine-tune only the reliability-bounded
fusion gate.
During online deployment, DATP-Net directly consumes
the latest incomplete telemetry window and its mask, without
requiring a separate imputation module. At each time slot, it
estimates the latent traffic state, activates the top-Kr experts,
retrieves reliable historical cases, and fuses the two predictions
as summarized in Algorithm 2. The output forecast is then
passed to downstream operation modules, such as SLA-aware
provisioning and traffic engineering.
V. EVALUATION
We evaluate DATP-Net along accuracy, generalization, ro-
bustness, trend preservation, routing behavior, operational
utility, ablation, and hyperparameter sensitivity. The research
questions are:
• RQ1: Forecasting accuracy vs. state-of-the-art methods?
• RQ2: Generalization to hydrological time series?
• RQ3: Robustness under Random and Structured Missing
Observations?
• RQ4: Trend preservation?
• RQ5: Distribution-guided expert routing behavior?
• RQ6: Translation into operational benefits (SLA provi-
sioning, TE)?
• RQ7: Component ablation, including Student-t vs. Gaus-
sian?
• RQ8: Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis.
Datasets. We evaluate DATP-Net on two real-world net-
work traffic datasets and three hydrological datasets. (1) Abi-
lene [31] records traffic demands among 12 nodes in the U.S.
Abilene backbone network, sampled every 5 minutes over 24
weeks. (2) G ´EANT [13] contains traffic measurements among
23 nodes in the pan-European G´EANT backbone network,
sampled every 15 minutes over 4 months. (3) To evaluate the
cross-domain robustness of DATP-Net beyond network traffic
forecasting, we additionally use three hydrological datasets
[32] containing hourly reservoir water-level observations col-
lected in Santa Clara County, California. Among the five
reservoirs in the original dataset, we select Almaden, Coyote,
and Lexington, using their records from 1991 to 2019.
Baselines. We compare DATP-Net with eleven represen-
tative time-series forecasting models covering Transformer-
based, recurrent, feature-mixing, and multi-scale architectures.
PMDformer [22] decouples patch-level mean information
from residual shape patterns, while HMformer [23] captures
temporal dependencies through hierarchical multi-scale mod-
eling. FEDformer [19], PatchTST [20], and iTransformer
[21] represent strong Transformer-based baselines that model
temporal dependencies through frequency-enhanced attention,
patch-wise representations, and variable-wise tokenization,
respectively. TimesNet [26] transforms one-dimensional se-
quences into multi-period two-dimensional representations,
whereas FeTS [28] and WPMixer [27] enhance forecasting
through feature-aware representation learning and wavelet-
based feature mixing. We also include several recent recurrent
forecasting models, including P-sLSTM [18], which combines
patching with structured LSTM memory, xLSTMTime [16],

---PAGE 9---
which adapts xLSTM to time-series forecasting, and xLSTM-
Mixer [17], which integrates xLSTM-based sequence model-
ing with feature-mixing operations.

Implementation. All experiments are implemented in Py-
Torch on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. We use Adam with
learning rate 5 × 10−4, batch size 32, and train for at most
200 epochs with early stopping on the validation loss.
For Abilene and G´EANT, we set L = 96, with Hshort = 5,
Hlong = 20, and d ∈{256, 512}. For the hydrology datasets,
we set L = 360, with Hshort = 8, Hlong = 72, and
d ∈{64, 128, 256, 512}. Parameter sensitivity is discussed
in Section V-I. We set the fusion upper bound λmax = 0.1
limits the maximum contribution of the historical retrieval
branch. The trend-consistency weight is λtrend = 0.1. The

TE setup. We formulate traffic engineering as a multi-
commodity flow (MCF) problem. For each OD pair, we
first construct a candidate path set using the P-shortest-path
algorithm, with P = 4 paths per OD pair. The TE solver then
determines the traffic splitting ratios over these candidate paths
by minimizing the maximum link utilization. The optimization
problem is solved by Gurobi.

To answer RQ1, we evaluate DATP-Net on Abilene and
G´EANT under both short- and long-horizon settings. The
results are reported in Tables I and II. DATP-Net achieves the
best NMAE, NRMSE, and COS on both datasets and under
both forecasting horizons, showing consistent advantages in
point-wise accuracy and trend preservation.

long-horizon forecasting, DATP-Net still maintains clear
advantages: it reduces NMAE/NRMSE by 10.4%/7.4% on

Overall, these results answer RQ1 by showing that DATP-

B. Generalization to Hydrological Forecasting (RQ2)
To answer RQ2, we further evaluate DATP-Net on three
hydrological datasets, including Almaden, Coyote, and Lex-
ington. These datasets differ from network traffic in sampling
granularity, temporal dynamics, and generation mechanisms,
and are used to examine the cross-domain generalization
ability of DATP-Net. The results are also reported in Tables I
and II.
DATP-Net achieves the best results on all three metrics
across all hydrological datasets and both forecasting horizons.
Under short-horizon forecasting, DATP-Net reduces NMAE
by 36.0%, 28.6%, and 40.7% on Almaden, Coyote, and
Lexington, respectively, compared with the best baselines.
Under long-horizon forecasting, DATP-Net still obtains the
lowest NMAE/NRMSE and the highest COS on all three
datasets. These results show that the proposed distribution-
aware and trend-preserving design is not limited to network
traffic, but also generalizes well to other time-series domains,
thereby answering RQ2.

F. SLA-Aware Capacity Provisioning (RQ6)
To answer RQ6, we evaluate whether the forecasting results
can improve SLA-aware capacity provisioning. Following the
cost model in Section III, each model reserves capacity
according to its predicted demand and an over-provisioning
margin. We report the trade-off between SLA violation rate
and normalized over-provisioning cost in Fig. 5. A curve closer
to the lower-left corner indicates a better balance between
service reliability and capacity cost.
DATP-Net consistently achieves better trade-off curves on
both Abilene and G´EANT. Under the same SLA violation
target, DATP-Net requires less redundant capacity; under the
same capacity cost, it achieves a lower violation rate. This
improvement is important because capacity provisioning is
highly sensitive to underestimation during traffic growth and
peak periods. By better preserving traffic trends, DATP-Net
reduces the need for excessive safety margins and provides
more reliable support for capacity management.
This observation is also consistent with Theorem 1: at an
equal over-provisioning cost, a lower violation rate implies
a smaller one-sided under-prediction error (UPE in Eq. (8)).
The SLA–Cost curves therefore provide empirical evidence
that the trend-consistency objective mitigates the peak under-
estimation behavior that symmetric pointwise losses do not
explicitly penalize. Thus, the provisioning results answer RQ6
by showing that DATP-Net’s forecasting gains translate into
operational benefits.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed DATP-Net, an operation-aware traf-
fic forecasting framework for incomplete network telemetry.
Different from generic forecasting methods, DATP-Net di-
rectly models missing observations, preserves traffic evolu-
tion trends, and adapts prediction behavior to heterogeneous
traffic regimes. We formulated forecasting under incomplete
telemetry and analyzed its impact on SLA-aware provisioning
and traffic engineering, showing that average error alone
is insufficient to reflect operational utility. DATP-Net inte-
grates missing-aware trend encoding, multi-scale Student-t
state estimation, distribution-guided Top-K expert forecasting,
and reliability-bounded historical retrieval. These components
jointly improve robustness to missing telemetry, reduce over-
smoothing, and provide adaptive forecasting for different traf-
fic states. Experiments on Abilene, G´EANT, and three hydro-
logical datasets show that DATP-Net consistently outperforms
representative baselines. It reduces short-horizon NMAE by
up to 17.1%, improves long-horizon COS by up to 81.6%,
and remains robust under structured time-block missingness.
Downstream evaluations further demonstrate better SLA–Cost
trade-offs and MLU ratios closer to the ideal value, confirm-
ing that DATP-Net improves both forecasting accuracy and
operational decision quality.
