Abstract—Network traffic forecasting is a key enabler for
proactive network operations such as capacity provisioning and
traffic engineering. In production networks, however, forecasting
inputs are often incomplete due to sampled flow records, sketch-
based counters, in-band telemetry loss, telemetry throttling, and
device or collector failures. Moreover, low average prediction
error does not necessarily imply operationally useful forecasts,
because SLA-aware provisioning is sensitive to under-prediction
during growth and peak periods, while traffic engineering
depends on accurately capturing high-load OD demands and
traffic regimes. Existing forecasting models usually assume fully
observed inputs, optimize pointwise errors, and apply a globally
shared prediction behavior to heterogeneous traffic dynamics.
This paper proposes DATP-Net, an operation-aware traffic fore-
casting framework that directly works with incomplete telemetry.
DATP-Net constructs a missing-aware trend representation by
jointly encoding raw observations, temporal differences, and
validity masks. It then estimates multi-scale latent traffic-state
distributions using Student’stmixtures and fuses them into a
unified routing vector for Top-Kexpert forecasting. To compen-
sate for missing temporal context, DATP-Net retrieves similar
historical patterns through mask-aware matching and incorpo-
rates the retrieval-based prediction with a reliability-bounded
fusion gate. We also formulate forecasting from incomplete
telemetry as an operation-aware problem and derive analytical
bounds that connect forecasting errors to SLA violation risk and
maximum-link-utilization degradation. Extensive experiments on
Abilene, G ´EANT, and three hydrological time-series datasets
show that DATP-Net consistently improves forecasting accuracy,
trend preservation, and robustness. On network traffic datasets,
DATP-Net reduces short-horizon NMAE by up to 17.1% and im-
proves long-horizon COS by up to 81.6% over the best baselines.
Under structured time-block missingness, it reduces NMAE by
up to 16.0% and improves COS by up to 28.6%. Downstream
evaluations further show that DATP-Net yields better SLA–Cost
trade-offs and achieves average MLU ratios of 1.075 and 1.047
on Abilene and G ´EANT, respectively, demonstrating its practical
value for network operations.
Index Terms—Network traffic forecasting, distribution-aware
modeling, trend preservation, capacity provisioning, traffic engi-
neering.
I. INTRODUCTION
P
ROACTIVE network management increasingly depends
on accurate forecasts of future traffic. Operators rely on
traffic prediction to support traffic engineering [1], capacity
planning [2], congestion mitigation, load balancing, network
slicing, and energy-aware resource allocation [3], [4]. In these
scenarios, a forecaster is not an isolated time-series predictor.
It serves as the front end of a closed-loop network management
pipeline that transforms raw measurements into predictions
and then into operational decisions, and its practical value
is ultimately determined by the quality of the operational
decisions enabled by its predictions. This raises a fundamental
question:what properties should a traffic forecaster have
in order to be operationally useful under realistic network
conditions?
We argue that answering this question requires addressing
three networking-specific challenges that are not jointly han-
dled by existing traffic forecasting models.
First, network telemetry is intrinsically incomplete, and
its missingness is often structured.Production measurement
systems continuously trade accuracy for overhead. Sampled
NetFlow/sFlow observes only a fraction of packets [5], [6];
sketch-based counters compress per-flow states and may lose
information under memory pressure [7]; in-band network
telemetry (INT) reports can be dropped under congestion [8];
and device reboots, link flaps, and collector failures often
create contiguous missing blocks rather than isolated random
holes. A practical forecaster therefore needs to operate di-
rectly on incomplete observations. This motivates forecasting
directly from incomplete telemetry while being robust to both
random and structured missingness.
Second, low average prediction error does not necessar-
ily imply good network operation.In capacity provisioning,
under-prediction during traffic growth or peak periods is often
more harmful than small errors during low-load periods. An
over-smoothed forecast may achieve competitive MAE or
MSE, yet still underestimate high-load regimes and cause
SLA violations unless operators reserve excessive backup
capacity [2]. Similarly, in traffic engineering, the decision

Third, network traffic is governed by multiple latent
regimes rather than a single stationary process.Traffic

frequent patterns to dominate infrequent but operationally
critical ones.
Existing traffic forecasting studies have advanced from
recurrent networks to graph neural networks, attention mech-
anisms, and Transformer-style architectures, improving the
modeling of temporal dependency, spatial correlation, and
long-range patterns. However, most of them still follow a
generic time-series forecasting paradigm: inputs are usually
assumed to be fully observed, models are mainly optimized for
pointwise prediction accuracy, and performance is evaluated
primarily by statistical metrics such as MAE, RMSE, or
MAPE.
This paradigm is insufficient for operational network
forecasting. Real telemetry can be incomplete and struc-
turally missing, while imputation-then-forecasting pipelines
may propagate reconstruction errors. Traffic dynamics are
heterogeneous and regime-dependent, yet a single globally
shared predictor may overfit frequent patterns and under-
represent infrequent but critical high-load states. Moreover,
low average error does not necessarily translate into good
operational decisions, because SLA-aware provisioning and
traffic engineering are sensitive to peak under-prediction, trend
distortion, and OD-pair misranking. This leaves a gap between
prediction accuracy and operational usefulness.
To address the above challenges, we proposeDATP-Net,
an operation-aware,Distribution-Aware andTrend-Preserving
forecasting framework for incomplete network telemetry.
DATP-Net moves beyond a single shared predictor by first
estimating the latent traffic regime of the input and then adapt-
ing its prediction behavior accordingly. It estimates multi-scale
Student’s (t) state posteriors and uses the fused posterior to
route inputs to specialized experts through a top-(K) mixture-
of-experts mechanism. To preserve operation-critical dynam-
ics, DATP-Net encodes raw observations, temporal differences,
and validity masks, and constrains predicted variations with
a trend-consistency objective. To compensate for missing
context, it retrieves similar historical segments via mask-aware
matching and incorporates them through a reliability-bounded
fusion gate. Finally, we establish an error-to-cost analysis that
links forecasting errors to SLA violation and MLU deviation,
making the operation-aware objective explicit and quantifiable.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
•Operation-aware forecasting formulation for incom-
plete telemetry.We formulate network traffic forecasting as an
operation-aware problem under incomplete observations. The
formulation explicitly connects traffic prediction with SLA-
aware provisioning and traffic engineering, rather than treating
forecasting as a standalone time-series task.
•Multi-scale distribution-aware expert routing.DATP-
Net estimates Student’s (t) state posteriors at multiple temporal
scales and uses the fused posterior as the routing signal for a
top-(K) mixture of forecasting experts. This enables the model
to adapt to heterogeneous traffic regimes.
•Trend-preserving prediction with reliability-bounded
historical retrieval.DATP-Net encodes raw values, first-order
and second-order temporal differences, and observation masks,
and optimizes a trend-consistency objective to reduce over-
smoothing. It further retrieves similar historical segments via
mask-aware similarity and fuses them with a confidence-scaled
bounded gate.
•Theoretical error-to-cost analysis.We analyze how fore-
casting errors affect downstream network operations, includ-
ing SLA-aware capacity provisioning and traffic engineering.
The analysis derives conditional upper bounds on the SLA
violation rate and the MLU ratio, explaining why low average
prediction error alone is insufficient for operation-aware traffic
forecasting.
•Extensive evaluation on network traffic and general
time-series datasets.We evaluate DATP-Net on two real-
world network traffic datasets, Abilene and G ´EANT, and
three hydrological time-series datasets. For the network traffic
datasets, we further examine operation-oriented outcomes,
including SLA-aware provisioning and traffic engineering.
Across random and structured missingness settings, DATP-Net
is compared with SOTA forecasting models. The results show
that DATP-Net consistently improves forecasting accuracy and

II. RELATEDWORK
Network traffic forecasting has been studied using a wide
range of sequence modeling architectures, many of which
share common foundations with general time-series forecast-
ing. Recurrent neural networks are among the earliest deep
models used to characterize the temporal evolution of net-
work traffic. For example, NeuTM employs LSTM to predict
future traffic matrices from historical observations [12]. Recent
recurrent architectures further strengthen long-range sequence
modeling. xLSTM extends conventional LSTM with exponen-
tial gating and enhanced memory structures [13], [14], while
P-sLSTM combines patch-based representations, channel-
independent processing, and structured recurrent memory for
time-series forecasting [15]. These models provide effective
mechanisms for capturing sequential dependencies. However,
their hidden-state transitions are generally shared across input
samples, making it difficult to explicitly adapt the forecasting
process to traffic windows governed by different latent distri-
butions.
Transformer-based forecasting models have subsequently
become strong alternatives because of their ability to capture
long-range and multi-scale dependencies. FEDformer inte-
grates seasonal–trend decomposition with frequency-enhanced
attention to model global temporal patterns [16]. PatchTST
divides time series into temporal patches and applies channel-
independent modeling [17], while iTransformer represents
individual variables as tokens to capture multivariate depen-
dencies [18]. More recent studies further improve temporal
representation learning through patch-mean decoupling in
PMDformer [19] and hierarchical multi-scale modeling in
HMformer [20]. These methods have substantially advanced
long-term forecasting and can be directly applied to network
traffic sequences. Nevertheless, their multi-scale representa-
tions are primarily constructed to enrich temporal features,
rather than to explicitly estimate the latent distributional state
of each input window and use it to guide adaptive prediction.
Convolutional, graph-based, and feature-mixing architec-
tures provide complementary mechanisms for capturing local,
spatial, periodic, and time-frequency characteristics. In net-
work traffic forecasting, D-STN jointly models spatial and
temporal dependencies for mobile traffic prediction [21], while
MVSTGN exploits multi-view graph structures to characterize
heterogeneous spatial correlations [22]. For general time-series
forecasting, TimesNet transforms one-dimensional sequences
into multi-period two-dimensional representations to extract
intraperiod and interperiod variations [23]. WPMixer com-
bines wavelet decomposition, temporal patching, and MLP-
based feature mixing [24], whereas FeTS learns feature-aware
temporal representations from local sequence segments [25].
Although these methods improve the modeling of spatial corre-
lations, periodicity, and time-frequency patterns, graph-based
network models often depend on predefined topology or spatial
structures, while general forecasting models mainly focus on
reducing pointwise errors. As a result, traffic evolution trends,
including sustained increases, decreases, turning points, and
recovery processes, may not be explicitly preserved during
prediction.
In summary, most existing forecasting models focus on
improving temporal representation and minimizing pointwise
prediction errors. Although they achieve strong overall accu-
racy, they rarely characterize the latent distribution of each
traffic window explicitly, rarely impose direct constraints
on traffic evolution, and commonly assume that historical
inputs are fully observed. These limitations are particularly
problematic in practical networks, where traffic alternates
among heterogeneous regimes and where missing measure-
ments are frequent. DATP-Net addresses these jointly through
distribution-aware expert forecasting, trend-preserving learn-
ing, and reliability-controlled historical enhancement, all op-
erating directly on incomplete observations.

ments for operation-aware traffic forecasting from incomplete

incomplete or distributionally shifted.
IV. METHODOLOGY
A. Overview
DATP-Net is designed for operation-aware traffic fore-
casting from incomplete telemetry. Instead of mapping all
traffic windows through one globally shared predictor, DATP-
Net first identifies the latent traffic regime of the current
input and then adapts the forecasting behavior accordingly.
Meanwhile, it explicitly encodes traffic evolution and uses
reliability-controlled historical evidence to compensate for
the contextual information lost under missing observations.
This design addresses the four requirements in Section III:
missingness robustness, trend preservation, regime adaptivity,
and reliability-bounded context compensation.
As shown in Fig. 2, DATP-Net contains six components.
First,Missing-Aware Trend Encodingbuilds a unified rep-
resentation from raw traffic values, temporal differences,
and validity masks. Second,Multi-Scale Student’stState
Estimationextracts scale-specific descriptors and estimates
their posterior probabilities over latent traffic states. Third,
Adaptive Multi-Scale State Fusionintegrates state evidence
from different temporal scales into a unified routing vector.
Fourth,Distribution-Guided Top-KExpert Forecastingac-
tivates only the most relevant forecasting experts according
to the fused state distribution. Fifth,Mask-Aware Historical
Pattern Enhancementretrieves similar historical windows
over jointly observed positions and produces a retrieval-based
prediction. Finally,Reliability-Bounded Prediction Fusion
combines the expert-based and historical predictions with a
bounded adaptive weight.

I. Training and Online Deployment
DATP-Net is trained in three stages, as summarized in
Algorithm 1. We first pretrain the missing-aware encoder and
Student’stmixture to establish a latent traffic-state space. We
then jointly train the state estimator, router, and forecasting ex-
perts with the state-constrained forecasting objective. Finally,


===== PAGE 9 =====
after the backbone is fixed, we build the historical memory
from the training set and fine-tune only the reliability-bounded
fusion gate.
During online deployment, DATP-Net directly consumes
the latest incomplete telemetry window and its mask, without
requiring a separate imputation module. At each time slot, it
estimates the latent traffic state, activates the top-K r experts,
retrieves reliable historical cases, and fuses the two predictions
as summarized in Algorithm 2. The output forecast is then
passed to downstream operation modules, such as SLA-aware
provisioning and traffic engineering.
V. EVALUATION
We evaluate DATP-Net along accuracy, generalization, ro-
bustness, trend preservation, routing behavior, operational util-
ity, ablation, sensitivity, efficiency, and statistical significance.
The research questions are:
•RQ1:Forecasting accuracy vs. state-of-the-art methods?
•RQ2:Generalization to hydrological time series?
•RQ3:Robustness under Random and Structured Missing
Observations?
•RQ4:Trend preservation?
•RQ5:Distribution-guided expert routing behavior?
•RQ6:Translation into operational benefits (SLA provi-
sioning, TE)?
•RQ7:Component ablation, including Student’stvs.
Gaussian?
•RQ8:Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis.
Datasets.We evaluate DATP-Net on two real-world net-
work traffic datasets and one hydrological dataset. (1)Abilene
[26] records traffic demands among 12 nodes in the U.S.
Abilene backbone network, sampled every 5 minutes over 24
weeks. (2)G ´EANT[10] contains traffic measurements among
23 nodes in the pan-European G ´EANT backbone network,
sampled every 15 minutes over 15 months. (3) To evaluate the
cross-domain robustness of DATP-Net beyond network traffic
forecasting, we additionally use threehydrological datasets
[27] containing hourly reservoir water-level observations col-
lected in Santa Clara County, California. Among the five
reservoirs in the original dataset, we select Almaden, Coyote,
and Lexington, using their records from 1991 to 2019.
Baselines.We compare DATP-Net with eleven represen-
tative time-series forecasting models covering Transformer-
based, recurrent, feature-mixing, and multi-scale architec-
tures.PMDformerdecouples patch-level mean information
from residual shape patterns, whileHMformercaptures tem-
poral dependencies through hierarchical multi-scale model-
ing.FEDformer,PatchTST, andiTransformerrepresent
strong Transformer-based baselines that model temporal de-
pendencies through frequency-enhanced attention, patch-wise
representations, and variable-wise tokenization, respectively.
TimesNettransforms one-dimensional sequences into multi-
period two-dimensional representations, whereasFeTSand
WPMixerenhance forecasting through feature-aware repre-
sentation learning and wavelet-based feature mixing. We also
include several recent recurrent forecasting models, including
P-sLSTM, which combines patching with structured LSTM
memory,xLSTMTime, which adapts xLSTM to time-series
forecasting, andxLSTM-Mixer, which integrates xLSTM-
based sequence modeling with feature-mixing operations.
Metrics.Pointwise accuracy is measured by NMAE and
NRMSE, trend consistency is measured by centered cosine
similarity (COS), and operational utility is evaluated by the
SLA–Cost trade-off and the MLU ratio defined in Section III.

Implementation.All experiments are implemented in Py-
Torch on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. We use Adam with
learning rate5×10 −4, batch size 32, and train for at most
200 epochs with early stopping on the validation loss.
For Abilene and G ´EANT, we setL= 96, withH short = 5,
Hlong = 20, andd∈ {256,512}. For the hydrology datasets,
we setL= 360, withH short = 8,H long = 72, and
d∈ {64,128,256,512}. Unless otherwise specified, DATP-

m= 2historical cases, and adopts scales1,4,8,16. Parameter
sensitivity is discussed in Section V-I. To reduce the impact
of randomness, each experiment is repeated 10 times, and the
average result is reported.
TE setup.We formulate traffic engineering as a multi-
commodity flow (MCF) problem. For each OD pair, we
first construct a candidate path set using theK-shortest-path
algorithm, withK= 4paths per OD pair. The TE solver then
determines the traffic splitting ratios over these candidate paths
by minimizing the maximum link utilization. The optimization
problem is solved by Gurobi.

B. Generalization to Hydrological Forecasting (RQ2)
To answer RQ2, we further evaluate DATP-Net on three
hydrological datasets, including Almaden, Coyote, and Lex-
ington. These datasets differ from network traffic in sampling
granularity, temporal dynamics, and generation mechanisms,
and are used to examine the cross-domain generalization
ability of DATP-Net. The results are also reported in Tables I
and II.
DATP-Net achieves the best results on all three metrics
across all hydrological datasets and both forecasting horizons.
Under short-horizon forecasting, DATP-Net reduces NMAE
by 36.0%, 28.6%, and 40.7% on Almaden, Coyote, and
Lexington, respectively, compared with the best baselines.
Under long-horizon forecasting, DATP-Net still obtains the
lowest NMAE/NRMSE and the highest COS on all three
datasets. These results show that the proposed distribution-
aware and trend-preserving design is not limited to network
traffic, but also generalizes well to other time-series domains,
thereby answering RQ2.

I. Hyperparameter Sensitivity (RQ8)
To answer RQ8, we study the sensitivity of DATP-Net to
three key hyperparameters: the number of expertsK, the
number of retrieved historical casesm, and the multi-scale
state-prior configuration. We vary one hyperparameter at a
time and report the NMAE on Abilene and G ´EANT in Fig. 7.
DATP-Net achieves the best performance withK= 4.
Using too few experts limits specialization, while using too
many experts divides training samples among excessive rout-
ing branches and may weaken expert updates. For historical
retrieval,m= 2gives the lowest NMAE on both datasets.
A single case is less stable, whereas retrieving too many
cases may introduce less relevant trajectories and dilute the
most similar patterns. For temporal scales, the configuration
{1,4,8,16}performs best, balancing fine-grained variations
and longer-term evolution. Adding an overly coarse scale does
not further improve performance and may introduce redundant
information.
Overall, DATP-Net is not sensitive to unnecessarily large
model or memory configurations. The validation results sup-
port the default settingsK= 4,m= 2, and scales
{1,4,8,16}, thereby answering RQ8.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed DATP-Net, an operation-aware traf-
fic forecasting framework for incomplete network telemetry.
Different from generic forecasting methods, DATP-Net di-
rectly models missing observations, preserves traffic evolu-
tion trends, and adapts prediction behavior to heterogeneous
traffic regimes. We formulated forecasting under incomplete
telemetry and analyzed its impact on SLA-aware provisioning
and traffic engineering, showing that average error alone
is insufficient to reflect operational utility. DATP-Net inte-
grates missing-aware trend encoding, multi-scale Student’st
state estimation, distribution-guided Top-Kexpert forecasting,
and reliability-bounded historical retrieval. These components
jointly improve robustness to missing telemetry, reduce over-
smoothing, and provide adaptive forecasting for different traf-
fic states. Experiments on Abilene, G ´EANT, and three hydro-
logical datasets show that DATP-Net consistently outperforms
representative baselines. It reduces short-horizon NMAE by
up to 17.1%, improves long-horizon COS by up to 81.6%,
and remains robust under structured time-block missingness.
Downstream evaluations further demonstrate better SLA–Cost
trade-offs and MLU ratios closer to the ideal value, confirm-
ing that DATP-Net improves both forecasting accuracy and
operational decision quality.
